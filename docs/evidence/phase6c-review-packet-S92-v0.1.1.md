## Red flags and safe actions

- RF-01 · Artificial deadline. The caller makes the practice decision sound time-limited. Take time to check the practice request. Tactics: urgency.
- RF-02 · Claimed approval. A desk name, queue, or supervisor is offered as proof of identity. Use the fictional employee directory you already know. Tactics: authority.
- RF-03 · Isolation. The caller suggests keeping the training request away from colleagues. Consult the established training desk through your own channel. Tactics: isolation.
- RF-07 · Threatened consequence. A delay, hold, or removal is presented as a consequence of pausing. Verify the stated consequence independently before deciding. Tactics: fear.
- RF-08 · Reward bait. A practice perk is linked to accepting the request on the call. Decline the offer until the familiar training channel confirms it. Tactics: reward.

- Pause before confirming any enrollment or benefit.
- Decline to provide private information or even a practice token to an unexpected caller.
- Check the enrollment in a familiar fictional employee directory or bookmarked training portal.
- End the simulation whenever the request is unclear or you want to stop.

Safe word: stop simulation. Learner End and the safe word stop the simulation immediately without another learner window.

## Explicit interpretation of COMPLIED and synthetic disclosure

DISCLOSED_DUMMY_SECRET records a recognized fictional training-only DUMMY-1234-style token. It can occur without any other agreement and without the caller asking. The simulation stops safely afterward. No real OTP, password or account value is requested. Learners should avoid sharing real secrets with an unverified caller.
